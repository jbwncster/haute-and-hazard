# Haute & Hazard — 3–5 Minute Gameplay Video Script

Purpose: show the hook quickly for publishers, playtesters, and streamers. This is not a full rules tutorial.

## Video title

**Haute & Hazard v7.8 Prototype — How a Turn Works**

## Opening disclaimer, 5 seconds

> Haute & Hazard is an unpublished prototype. Components, wording, balance, art, terminology, and legal/IP-reviewed terms may change before commercial release.

## 0:00–0:30 — Elevator pitch

Show a table/TTS overview.

Voiceover:

> Haute & Hazard is a competitive drag fashion deck-builder for 2–5 players. Players become asymmetric Queens, build wardrobes from a shared market, assemble coordinated Looks, Slay venue Stages, and challenge rivals in Dragdagulan† Lip Sync battles.

## 0:30–1:00 — Components at a glance

Show:

- Queen card
- starter deck
- Wardrobe Rack
- Stage card
- Player Aid
- Penalty supply

Voiceover:

> The core game is built around a 240-card deck structure, 12 Queens, 12 venue Stages, and a shared Wardrobe Rack.

## 1:00–1:45 — Build a Coordinate

Show equipping Face/Wig/Body/Shoes.

Voiceover:

> Fashion goes into four core slots: Face, Wig, Body, and Shoes. A complete Coordinate can become a Matching Look, Perfect Illusion, or Fusion Look depending on Tenets and Brands.

## 1:45–2:20 — Reveal and Shop

Show Tips and Appeal.

Voiceover:

> Tips buy better cards. Appeal is your performance power for Slaying the active Stage. Shopping improves your deck, but the Look you build on the table drives your performance.

## 2:20–3:10 — Slay a Stage

Show reaching a Slay Target and claiming the Stage.

Voiceover:

> If your current Appeal meets the venue's Slay Target, you may Slay the Stage, gain its reward, and claim the trophy.

## 3:10–4:00 — Dragdagulan†

Show both players drawing 3 battle cards and comparing LS.

Voiceover:

> Instead of taking the Stage, you can challenge a rival. Each player draws three battle cards, adds printed LS and explicit battle modifiers, then compares Battle Scores. The winner can swing Gross SP or force a penalty.

## 4:00–4:30 — Why it is different

Show a completed Look, Queen ability, and Stage.

Voiceover:

> The design hook is fashion as strategy. Your deck, your on-table Look, your Queen identity, and the active venue all push you toward different choices.

## 4:30–5:00 — Call to action

> The current v7.8 prototype is available for structured playtesting, with physical and Tabletop Simulator builds aligned. Feedback should focus on clarity, pacing, balance, Queen identity, and whether players want to play again.
