# Haute & Hazard — Beginner Mode

**Applies to the current v7.8 Game Crafter Print Edition.**

Beginner Mode is the recommended way to teach a first physical game of **Haute & Hazard**. It uses the normal Queens, starter decks, Wardrobe Rack, Thrift Store, Penalties, Shopping economy, Look rules, Slay rules, and Dragdagulan rules. The only change is that the Stage card is read in a simplified way.

No cards need to be removed, replaced, covered, or reprinted.

## What Beginner Mode changes

When a Stage becomes active, use only these five parts of the Stage:

1. **Favored Tenet**
2. **Featured Brand**
3. **Slay Target**
4. **Reward**
5. **Venue Effect**

For the entire Beginner Mode game, ignore these four advanced Stage features:

- **Judge**
- **Spotlight Requirement**
- **Judge's Favor**
- **Brand Ovation**

If a piece of Stage text only exists to explain one of those ignored advanced features, ignore that text as well.

## What stays exactly the same

Beginner Mode does **not** simplify the rest of the game. Players still learn the systems that make Haute & Hazard work:

- the Pink / Blue / Purple / Yellow / Neutral **Tenet** system;
- named **Brands** within those Tenets;
- the four core Coordinate slots: Face, Wig, Body, and Shoes;
- Complete Coordinates;
- Matching Looks and Perfect Illusions;
- Fusion Looks;
- **Tips** and the Shopping economy;
- Wardrobe Rack and Thrift Store purchases;
- **Appeal** and Slaying the active Stage;
- **LS** and Dragdagulan;
- Penalties;
- Queen Signature Abilities;
- Queen **Special Appeal — Once Per Game**;
- Gross Style Points and the normal end-game trigger.

The purpose of Beginner Mode is not to create a different game. It removes one layer of Stage scoring so new players can focus on building a Coordinate, buying cards, understanding Tenets/Brands, and choosing between Slay and Dragdagulan.

## Recommended teaching setup

Beginner Mode works with the normal **2–5 player** rules. For the easiest first teaching game, use **2 players**.

Each player receives the standard 12-card Dressing Room Floor starter deck:

- 7 × **Basic Beat**
- 3 × **Messy Lip Sync**
- 2 × **Chapstick**

Shuffle the normal 12-card venue Stage deck. When the first Stage is revealed, point out only the five Beginner Mode fields and tell players to disregard the Judge/Spotlight area for this game.

All other setup follows the current rules.

## How to read a Stage in Beginner Mode

Read the active Stage in this order:

### 1. Favored Tenet

This tells players which Tenet the venue rewards or interacts with. A Tenet means the card's **color**, not its named Brand.

### 2. Featured Brand

This points players toward a specific Brand represented at that venue. Unlike a Tenet check, a Brand check requires the exact named Brand.

In Beginner Mode, Featured Brand matters only when the Stage's Venue Effect or another active card explicitly checks it. **Brand Ovation is ignored.**

### 3. Slay Target

This is the amount of Appeal required to Slay the Stage, subject to any normal prerequisite or cost printed outside the ignored advanced Stage area.

### 4. Reward

If the player successfully Slays the Stage, they claim the Stage trophy and gain its printed reward as normal.

### 5. Venue Effect

Resolve the venue's normal effect exactly as written unless that text specifically depends on Judge, Spotlight Requirement, Judge's Favor, or Brand Ovation. In that case, ignore only the advanced-dependent portion.

## Example

A Stage might show:

**NEON NIGHTCLUB**  
Favored Tenet: Pink  
Featured Brand: Hyper-Glitch  
Slay Target: 12 Appeal  
Reward: 3 SP  
Venue Effect: Hyper-Glitch Fashion gains +1 Appeal during the Reveal.

It may also show a Judge, Spotlight Requirement, Judge's Favor, and Brand Ovation.

In Beginner Mode, play the Stage using only the first five items. A player can benefit from the Hyper-Glitch Venue Effect and Slay at 12 Appeal for the printed reward. The Judge and all rewards tied specifically to Spotlight/Judge's Favor/Brand Ovation are simply inactive.

## Turn structure in Beginner Mode

The normal five phases do not change:

1. **Transformation** — equip Fashion, play Actions, and use eligible Queen abilities.
2. **The Reveal** — total resources, resolve Look states and abilities, and determine Appeal.
3. **Shopping** — spend Tips on legal Wardrobe Rack and Thrift Store purchases.
4. **Slay / Dragdagulan / Pass** — choose exactly one unless an effect says otherwise.
5. **Cleanup** — resolve normal cleanup, draw a new hand, and reset temporary resources.

## Slaying a Stage

To Slay in Beginner Mode:

1. Reach or exceed the Stage's printed **Slay Target**.
2. Satisfy any normal prerequisite or cost that is not part of the ignored Judge/Spotlight layer.
3. Claim the Stage trophy.
4. Gain the printed Stage reward.
5. Resolve normal Slay effects and Curtain Call.

Do **not** check Spotlight Requirement, award Judge's Favor, or award Brand Ovation.

## Dragdagulan is unchanged

Beginner Mode does not alter Dragdagulan.

Each player in the battle draws **3 cards** from their personal Deck. Battle Score is the sum of the printed **LS** on those three battle-drawn cards plus explicit active LS modifiers.

Equipped Fashion does not automatically add its printed LS to Battle Score.

## Moving to the full Stage rules

After players understand the basic loop, play the next game with the full Stage card active.

Re-enable:

- Judge
- Spotlight Requirement
- Judge's Favor
- Brand Ovation

Nothing else changes. The same Stage deck, Queens, starter decks, and card pool are used. Players are simply adding the venue's advanced scoring/objective layer back into the game.

This makes Beginner Mode a teaching layer rather than a separate ruleset.

## Recommended teaching explanation

A first-time player only needs this Stage summary:

> **Match the fashion, build enough Appeal, and Slay the venue. The Judge stuff comes in your next game.**

## Beginner Mode quick reference

**USE:** Favored Tenet • Featured Brand • Slay Target • Reward • Venue Effect  
**IGNORE:** Judge • Spotlight Requirement • Judge's Favor • Brand Ovation

Everything else in Haute & Hazard plays normally.
