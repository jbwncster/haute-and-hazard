# Haute & Hazard — Rulebook Lore Thread

**Purpose:** a light, continuous narrative to weave through the printed rules without changing gameplay.

The current Game Crafter **Rules** component is a **Medium Folio Set**. This document is the canonical lore-placement plan for the next rulebook regeneration.

> **Lore callouts have no rules effect.** If flavor and rules ever conflict, the rules win.

## The mini-lore — The Haute Circuit

Once each year, the city becomes a runway.

For one night, twelve notorious venues open their doors to the Queens of **The Haute Circuit**. The rooms range from glamorous to strange, expensive to questionable, and every one of them has its own crowd, taste, favorite Brands, and idea of what deserves applause.

No Queen arrives finished.

Each begins backstage with a modest Dressing Room Floor deck, a signature point of view, and enough nerve to believe the night might belong to her. Between venues, applause becomes **Tips**. Tips become new Fashion. Fashion becomes a stronger **Coordinate**. A stronger Coordinate becomes **Appeal**. Appeal becomes trophies, Style Points, and reputation.

But the Circuit is not a solo performance. Other Queens want the same cards, the same rooms, and the same headlines. Rivalries spill into Lip Sync battles. Wardrobe Malfunctions become gossip. A bad Chop can follow someone all night. A perfect performance can turn a Queen into the name everyone is suddenly whispering.

Each **Curtain Call** closes one chapter of the night and sends the cast toward the next venue.

When the final room closes—or one Queen has built enough momentum—the Circuit ends in **Final Judging**. Trophies, Style Points, penalties, rivalries, and unforgettable moments are counted together.

The winner is not simply the Queen who wore the most expensive pieces.

She is the Queen the city remembers when the lights come back on.

## Recurring rulebook device — Backstage Beat

Use short italic callouts headed **Backstage Beat**. Read in order, they form the miniature story above while keeping the rulebook rules-first.

The voice should feel like a knowing backstage narrator: campy, theatrical, concise, and a little dangerous. The narrator remains unnamed so a publisher can later turn the voice into a character, emcee, announcer, gossip columnist, or other framing device if desired.

### 1. Opening / game objective — The Invitation

> **Backstage Beat — The Invitation**  
> Once a year, twelve venues light up for the Haute Circuit. Every Queen receives the same invitation: arrive with what you have, leave with a reputation. By sunrise, the city will have chosen the performance it cannot forget.

**Story beat:** The player enters the Circuit.  
**Rules beat:** Explain the goal, Gross SP, Stage trophies, and Final Judging.

### 2. Setup — Dressing Room Floor

> **Backstage Beat — Start With What You've Got**  
> Nobody enters the Circuit fully dressed. Your first deck is foundation, nerve, and whatever made it through the dressing-room door. The Look that defines your night is still somewhere out there.

**Story beat:** The Queen begins unfinished.  
**Rules beat:** Starting deck, Queen, Stage deck, Wardrobe Rack, Thrift Store, penalties, Player Aid.

### 3. Choosing a Queen — A Point of View

> **Backstage Beat — Every Queen Has a Point of View**  
> A Queen is more than a costume. She has a signature, a favorite place to shop, and one trick the room may never see coming. Follow her instincts—or make the night prove she can become something else.

**Story beat:** The player chooses an identity.  
**Rules beat:** Signature Tenet, Favorite Brand, Signature Ability, Special Appeal.

### 4. Coordinate — Building the Silhouette

> **Backstage Beat — The Mirror Is Not Finished Yet**  
> Face. Wig. Body. Shoes. Piece by piece, the silhouette comes together. The first card is an idea. A Complete Coordinate is a statement.

**Story beat:** The Queen starts becoming visible.  
**Rules beat:** Masters, core slots, Accessory, Deep Storage, Complete Coordinate.

### 5. Tenets and Brands — Learning the Fashion Language

> **Backstage Beat — A Look Says Something**  
> Tenets are the broad language of the Look. Brands are the dialect. Matching says you committed. Perfect Illusion says the Look belongs to you. Fusion says you knew exactly which rules to break.

**Story beat:** The Queen develops a recognizable fashion identity.  
**Rules beat:** Tenets, Brands, Matching Look, Perfect Illusion, Fusion.

### 6. Transformation — Five Minutes to Places

> **Backstage Beat — Five Minutes to Places**  
> Backstage is controlled panic: wigs pinned, shoes changed, makeup repaired, grudges renewed. Transformation is where the next version of your Queen takes shape.

**Story beat:** The Queen prepares for the next room.  
**Rules beat:** Equipping Fashion, Actions, Queen abilities, timing.

### 7. The Reveal — Lights Up

> **Backstage Beat — Lights Up**  
> The curtain parts. A pile of cards becomes a Look, a set of choices becomes Appeal, and suddenly everyone in the room can see whether the fantasy holds together.

**Story beat:** Preparation becomes performance.  
**Rules beat:** Resource generation, Wild Tenets, Look states, Appeal multipliers.

### 8. Shopping — Spend the Applause

> **Backstage Beat — Spend the Applause**  
> Applause becomes Tips, and Tips never stay quiet for long. The Wardrobe Rack is a feeding frenzy: better pieces, stranger pieces, and the one card another Queen was absolutely planning to buy.

**Story beat:** Success funds transformation.  
**Rules beat:** Tips, Wardrobe Rack, Backstage Archive, market refill.

### 9. Thrift Store — The Unexpected Find

> **Backstage Beat — Couture Has Many Origins**  
> Not every legendary Look arrives in a garment bag. Sometimes the piece that saves the night was already discarded by somebody who lacked imagination.

**Story beat:** The Queen learns that status and style are not the same thing.  
**Rules beat:** Throwbacks and Thrift Store purchases.

### 10. Stage — Read the Room

> **Backstage Beat — New Room, New Rules**  
> Every venue has its own crowd, its own taste, and its own idea of perfection. The Look that conquered the last room may be completely wrong for this one. Read the room before it reads you.

**Story beat:** The Circuit moves to a new venue.  
**Rules beat:** Favored Tenet, Featured Brand, Slay Target, Venue Effect, Judge, Spotlight, Brand Ovation.

### 11. Slay — Own the Moment

> **Backstage Beat — Take the Stage**  
> Enough Appeal turns preparation into a moment. Meet the room where it lives, give it more than it expected, and the Stage becomes part of your story.

**Story beat:** The Queen converts a Look into reputation.  
**Rules beat:** Slay requirements, rewards, Stage trophy, SP.

### 12. Dragdagulan† — Rivalry Becomes a Number

> **Backstage Beat — Some Rivalries Need Music**  
> Sometimes winning the room is not enough. One look across the floor, one name called into a microphone, and suddenly two Queens have to settle the question under the same lights.

**Story beat:** Competition becomes direct.  
**Rules beat:** Challenge eligibility, three battle cards, LS, winner reward, The Chop.

Keep the **† legal/IP marker** wherever the current working term appears.

### 13. Penalties — The Night Keeps Receipts

> **Backstage Beat — The Night Keeps Receipts**  
> A bad break can follow you from venue to venue. Wardrobe Malfunctions become stories, The Chop becomes gossip, and Extermination is very hard to pretend never happened.

**Story beat:** The night's failures become part of the Queen's reputation.  
**Rules beat:** Penalty cards and end-game deductions.

### 14. Curtain Call — Pack the Drama

> **Backstage Beat — Clear the Stage**  
> The lights come down. The room exhales. Whatever just happened becomes history before anyone has time to recover. Pack the Look, keep the gossip, and move. Another venue is already opening its doors.

**Story beat:** One chapter ends and the Circuit continues.  
**Rules beat:** Stage cleanup, reset, next venue, end-trigger checks.

### 15. Final Judging — What Will They Remember?

> **Backstage Beat — What Will They Remember?**  
> At the end of the Circuit, nobody remembers every stitch. They remember trophies, disasters, rivalries, impossible recoveries, and the Queen who made the whole night feel inevitable.

**Story beat:** The city's memory becomes the final score.  
**Rules beat:** Gross SP, bonuses, penalties, tiebreakers, winner.

### 16. Epilogue / back page — Sunrise

> **Backstage Beat — Sunrise**  
> The venues go dark. Makeup comes off. Shoes are abandoned under tables. By morning, every Queen has a story—but only one name is still moving from mouth to mouth.

**Story beat:** Close the book on a full night.  
**Rules beat:** None. This is optional back-page flavor.

## How the lore mirrors the gameplay loop

The rulebook story should make the game loop feel inevitable:

**Arrive unfinished → choose a point of view → build a Look → reveal it → earn Tips → shop → adapt to the next venue → Slay or confront a rival → carry the consequences → move on → face Final Judging.**

That same sequence explains why the major systems belong together rather than feeling like unrelated mechanics.

## Beginner Mode lore

Do not write separate lore for Beginner Mode. The fiction is identical; only the information layer changes.

A first-time player is still traveling the Haute Circuit. They simply ignore Judge, Spotlight Requirement, Judge's Favor, and Brand Ovation until they are ready for the full venue layer.

## Experimental two-placement lore

When testing the alternate Stage Winner / Runner-Up rules, use this replacement Curtain Call transition:

> **Backstage Beat — One More Number**  
> The winner has taken the room, but the lights are still on. There is time for one more Queen to prove she belonged in the conversation—and maybe call somebody out before the curtain falls.

This module remains non-canonical until playtesting supports it.

## Placement guidance for the Medium Folio

Do **not** put a lore box on every rules paragraph. The callouts above are the full narrative pool; the printed folio should prioritize the beats that fit without sacrificing clarity.

Highest-priority placements:

1. opening/game objective — **The Invitation**;
2. setup — **Start With What You've Got**;
3. Queen/Coordinate — **Every Queen Has a Point of View** or **The Mirror Is Not Finished Yet**;
4. Tenets/Brands/Look states — **A Look Says Something**;
5. turn structure — **Five Minutes to Places** and/or **Lights Up**;
6. Shopping — **Spend the Applause**;
7. Stage/Slay — **New Room, New Rules** and **Take the Stage**;
8. Dragdagulan† — **Some Rivalries Need Music**;
9. Curtain Call — **Clear the Stage**;
10. Final Judging — **What Will They Remember?**;
11. back page if space allows — **Sunrise**.

Keep most callouts to roughly **20–50 words**. They should be visually distinct, easy to skip, and never interrupt a procedure, table, example, or timing sequence.

## Publisher-development note

The lore intentionally avoids locking the game into named non-player characters, a specific city, or a fixed canon protagonist. A publisher can deepen the setting later without rewriting the mechanical story.

The pieces worth preserving are:

- **The Haute Circuit** as the night-long structure;
- venues as chapters in the night;
- applause becoming Tips, then wardrobe, then performance;
- the Queen visibly transforming over the course of play;
- rivalry leaving consequences;
- Final Judging as the city's memory of the whole night.
