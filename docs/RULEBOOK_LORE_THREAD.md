# Haute & Hazard — Rulebook Lore Thread

**Purpose:** a light narrative thread to weave through the printed rules without changing gameplay.

The current Game Crafter **Rules** component is a Medium Folio Set. On the next rulebook regeneration, use these short callouts as connective tissue between rules sections.

> **Lore callouts have no rules effect.** If flavor and rules ever appear to conflict, the rules win.

## Core premise — The Haute Circuit

Once a year, the city's most notorious venues open their doors for **The Haute Circuit**: a single-night crawl of fashion, performance, rivalry, and reputation.

Each player is a Queen arriving with a modest Dressing Room Floor deck and a point of view. Across the night, Queens earn Tips, raid the Wardrobe Rack, build increasingly audacious Coordinates, take over venue Stages, and settle rivalries under the lights.

Every Stage is another stop on the Circuit. Every Slay adds to a Queen's reputation. Every penalty becomes backstage gossip. When the last venue closes—or one Queen has built enough momentum—the Circuit ends in **Final Judging**, where the whole night is remembered at once.

## Recurring rulebook device: Backstage Beat

Use short italic callouts headed **Backstage Beat**. They should sound like a knowing backstage narrator: campy, theatrical, concise, and secondary to the rules.

Do not name the narrator yet; leave room for publisher development.

### Game overview — Call Time

> **Backstage Beat — Call Time**  
> The city is dressed for trouble. Twelve venues are lit, the Wardrobe Rack is overflowing, and every Queen in the room believes the night belongs to her. Start with what you brought. By sunrise, everyone will know what you became.

### Setup — Dressing Room Floor

> **Backstage Beat — Start With What You've Got**  
> Nobody enters the Circuit fully dressed. Your first hand is foundation, nerve, and whatever survived the trip backstage. The rest of the Look is waiting somewhere under brighter lights.

### Queens

> **Backstage Beat — Every Queen Has a Point of View**  
> A Queen is more than a costume. Her signature tells you how she wants to own the night. You decide whether to follow that instinct or break character completely.

### Transformation

> **Backstage Beat — Five Minutes to Places**  
> Backstage is controlled panic: wigs pinned, shoes changed, makeup repaired, grudges renewed. Transformation is where the next version of your Queen takes shape.

### Reveal

> **Backstage Beat — Lights Up**  
> The curtain parts. A pile of cards becomes a Look, a collection of choices becomes Appeal, and suddenly everyone can see whether the fantasy holds together.

### Tenets and Brands

> **Backstage Beat — A Look Says Something**  
> Tenets are the broad language of the Look. Brands are the dialect. Matching says you committed. Perfect Illusion says the Look could only belong to you. Fusion says you knew exactly which rules to break.

### Shopping / Wardrobe Rack

> **Backstage Beat — Spend the Applause**  
> Tips do not stay in your pocket for long. Between performances, the Wardrobe Rack becomes a feeding frenzy: better pieces, stranger pieces, and the one card somebody else was clearly saving for.

### Thrift Store

> **Backstage Beat — Couture Has Many Origins**  
> Not every winning Look arrives in a garment bag. The right Queen can turn a forgotten Throwback into the piece everyone talks about tomorrow.

### Stages

> **Backstage Beat — New Room, New Rules**  
> Every venue has its own crowd, its own taste, and its own idea of what deserves applause. Read the room before you decide what to wear.

### Slay

> **Backstage Beat — Take the Stage**  
> Enough Appeal turns preparation into a moment. Hit the target, satisfy the room, and the Stage becomes part of your story.

### Dragdagulan†

> **Backstage Beat — Some Rivalries Need Music**  
> Sometimes the Stage is not enough. When one Queen calls out another, the room stops shopping and starts watching.

Keep the **† legal/IP marker** wherever the current working term appears.

### Penalties

> **Backstage Beat — The Night Keeps Receipts**  
> A bad break can follow you from venue to venue. Wardrobe Malfunctions become stories, The Chop becomes gossip, and Extermination is very hard to pretend never happened.

### Curtain Call

> **Backstage Beat — Clear the Stage**  
> The lights come down, the room exhales, and whatever just happened becomes history. Pack the drama. There is another venue waiting.

### Final Judging

> **Backstage Beat — What Will They Remember?**  
> At the end of the Circuit, nobody remembers every stitch. They remember the trophies, the disasters, the rivalries, and the Queen who made the whole night feel inevitable.

## Experimental two-placement lore

When testing the alternate Stage Winner / Runner-Up rules, use this replacement Curtain Call transition:

> **Backstage Beat — One More Number**  
> The winner has taken the room, but the lights are still on. There is time for one more Queen to prove she belonged in the conversation—and maybe call somebody out before the curtain falls.

## Placement guidance for the Medium Folio

Do not put a lore box on every rules block. A good target is **one Backstage Beat per major rules section or panel**.

Priority placements:

1. opening/game objective;
2. setup;
3. Coordinate / Tenets / Brands;
4. turn structure;
5. Shopping;
6. Stage / Slay;
7. Dragdagulan†;
8. Curtain Call / Final Judging.

Keep each callout to roughly 20–45 words so the Medium Folio remains a rules-first document.
