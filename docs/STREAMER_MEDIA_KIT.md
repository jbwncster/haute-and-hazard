## Short description

Haute & Hazard is a competitive drag fashion deck-building game for **2–5 players**. Players become asymmetric Queens, build wardrobes from a shared market, assemble four-piece fashion Coordinates, generate Tips to shop, build Appeal to Slay venue Stages, and challenge rivals in Dragdagulan† Lip Sync battles.

The current **v7.8 prototype is playable both physically and in Tabletop Simulator**, with the digital build aligned to the current physical rules, card pool, Stages, Queens, Player Aid wording, Beginner Mode, and core systems.

## One-sentence hook

**Build your deck. Assemble your look. Slay the Stage.**

## Current prototype snapshot

The current v7.8 playtest includes:

- **2–5 players**;
- a **240-card Poker Deck**: 60 starter / 144 Wardrobe / 16 Thrift / 20 Penalty;
- **12 venue-style Stages**, balanced across Pink, Blue, Purple, and Yellow Tenets;
- **12 asymmetric Queens**, each with a Signature Ability and a once-per-game Special Appeal;
- four core Coordinate slots: **Face, Wig, Body, and Shoes**, with Accessory optional;
- **Tips / Shopping**, Appeal, Matching Looks, Perfect Illusion, Fusion, Slay, and Dragdagulan†;
- **Beginner Mode** for first games and the full advanced Stage layer for experienced players;
- matching **physical Game Crafter** and **Tabletop Simulator** playtest targets.

## Design origin / inspiration talking point

If viewers ask where the game came from, a concise accurate answer is:

> Haute & Hazard is an original drag-centered tabletop deck-builder. One major structural inspiration is Bandai's Data Carddass Aikatsu! arcade game, especially the high-level idea of fashion pieces as gameplay objects with categories, Brands, coordination, and performance value. Haute & Hazard transforms that inspiration into its own slots, Tenets, Brands, Shopping economy, persistent Coordinate, Stages, Queens, scoring, and rivalry systems.

For the full design-history explanation, see [DESIGN_ORIGIN_AND_INSPIRATION.md](DESIGN_ORIGIN_AND_INSPIRATION.md).

## Useful talking points during a stream

- Players start from modest decks and **shop their way into a personal wardrobe**.
- Fashion is mechanically central: building a complete Coordinate changes how the player performs.
- **Tenets** are broad color/faction identities; **Brands** are named fashion lines within them.
- **Matching Look** rewards a coherent Tenet.
- **Perfect Illusion** rewards matching the Queen's Signature Tenet.
- **Fusion** rewards intentionally combining exactly two non-Neutral Tenets.
- Stages are **venues**, not generic challenge cards, and can change what players want to wear.
- Queens are asymmetric and each has a once-per-game **Special Appeal**.
- **Dragdagulan†** is the direct head-to-head Lip Sync battle system.
- The game is still being actively developed, so confusion, pacing issues, and balance problems are valuable playtest feedback.

## What to capture

Useful screenshots/video moments:

- the full physical table or TTS setup;
- Queen selection;
- the opening Wardrobe Rack;
- a completed Coordinate;
- a Matching Look, Perfect Illusion, or Fusion Look coming together;
- a Shopping decision;
- a Slay attempt;
- a Dragdagulan† battle;
- a Queen Special Appeal being used;
- an advanced Stage objective or Brand Ovation, if using the full Stage rules;
- Curtain Call;
- end-game Final Judging / final score.

For TTS footage, an overhead table view plus close-ups of the active Queen, Coordinate, Stage, and Wardrobe Rack will usually communicate the game most clearly.

## What feedback is most useful

Ask players:

1. What was the most fun moment?
2. What was the most confusing moment?
3. Did Shopping feel useful and competitive?
4. Did building a Coordinate feel satisfying?
5. Did the Stage change what you wanted to wear or buy?
6. Did your Queen feel distinct?
7. Did Dragdagulan† feel tense and worthwhile?
8. If you used the advanced Stage layer, did Judge / Spotlight / Brand Ovation add interesting decisions or too much information?
9. Did the physical or TTS interface make anything harder to understand than the rules themselves?
10. Would you play again?

For structured feedback, use [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md) or the GitHub playtest-report form.
