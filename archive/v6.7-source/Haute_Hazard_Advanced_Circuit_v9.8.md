## 1. INTRODUCTION TO ADVANCED PLAY

### The Ballroom Circuit

The Advanced Circuit transforms Haute & Hazard from a competitive deck-builder into a full ballroom simulation where players join houses, compete in judged categories, and navigate the complex social dynamics of ball culture. This expansion adds strategic depth through house drafting, dual-deck challenge systems, and resource management via tens tokens.

The Advanced Circuit is designed for players who have mastered the core gameplay loop and desire additional mechanical complexity with deeper strategic planning horizons. Games running Advanced Circuit rules typically extend to ninety minutes and support more nuanced interaction patterns between players competing for house supremacy.

### What Changes?

The Advanced Circuit modifies the core game in five significant ways. First, players draft Ball House cards during setup that provide persistent passive abilities and personal two-card vaults for strategic card banking. Second, the Category Deck runs parallel to the Stage Deck, creating dual requirement systems where players must satisfy both stage prerequisites and judging categories to earn tens tokens. Third, tens tokens function as a flexible resource economy that can be converted into Tips or Appeal at any moment. Fourth, Deep Storage becomes a more prominent mechanic with the addition of Vogue keyword cards that allow swapping positions during Phase 2. Fifth, the Perfect Illusion state gains enhanced significance by granting Untouchable status alongside triple Appeal multipliers.

### Prerequisites

Players should have completed at least five games using the Core Rulebook v6.7 before attempting the Advanced Circuit. Familiarity with Deep Storage mechanics, Fusion Look requirements, and the standard five-phase turn structure is essential. The Advanced Circuit assumes players can execute core gameplay without constant rule references and are ready for increased decision complexity.

## 2. ADDITIONAL COMPONENTS

### Ball House Cards (12 cards)

Each Ball House represents an iconic voguing house from ballroom history with unique passive abilities and vault specifications. Houses provide persistent benefits throughout the game and shape player strategy from the opening turns. The twelve houses include diverse mechanical identities spanning resource generation, combat bonuses, category advantages, and specialized synergies with specific brands or card types.

### Category Deck (12-15 cards)

Category cards present judging themes that run parallel to Stage challenges. Each Category specifies a requirement such as Executive Realness, Vogue Femme, Face Face Face, or Butch Queen Up In Drags. Meeting a Category requirement during Phase 2 awards one tens token. Categories remain active for the entire Round until replaced during the Grand Finale.

### Tens Tokens (40-50 tokens)

Physical tokens representing judging scores from the ballroom floor. Tens tokens accumulate over multiple Rounds as players successfully meet Category requirements. Players can redeem tens tokens at any time during their turn by spending them for immediate resources: one tens token converts to either $2 Tips or +2★ Appeal.

### House Vault Storage (player-specific)

Each player receives a small play area next to their Player Mat designated as their House Vault. The Vault can store up to two cards at any time, functioning as a strategic reserve that persists across Rounds even through Grand Finale resets.

## 3. ADVANCED SETUP: THE CHOICE

### Step 1: House Claim

Before any other setup procedures, shuffle all twelve Ball House cards together. Deal two House cards face-down to each player. Players examine both of their dealt Houses privately without revealing them to opponents.

### Step 2: The Selection

Each player selects one of their two dealt Houses to claim for the duration of the game. The chosen House is placed face-up in front of the player where all opponents can see it. The unchosen House is returned to the game box and will not be used this game.

This drafting process creates information asymmetry where each player knows they received specific house options but opponents do not know which houses were rejected. The Selection phase rewards players who can quickly evaluate house synergies with their chosen Queen character and predict which houses opponents likely selected.

### Step 3: Initialize House Vaults

Each player's chosen Ball House card specifies the Vault capacity and any special Vault rules. Most Houses provide a standard two-card Vault capacity. Some Houses may modify this capacity or impose restrictions on what card types can be stored in the Vault.

Players begin the game with empty Vaults. Cards cannot be placed into Vaults until gameplay begins and players gain access to cards through draw or purchase actions.

### Step 4: Standard Setup Continues

After House selection completes, proceed with the standard setup sequence from Core Rulebook v6.7: reveal the Active Stage, deal the Wardrobe Rack marketplace, distribute Dressing Room Floor starter decks, place token pools, and determine starting player.

### Step 5: Opening the Ball

After all standard setup completes, perform the additional Opening the Ball procedure. Shuffle the Category Deck and place it face-down near the Stage Deck. Flip the top Category card face-up to establish the active judging requirement for Round 1. This Category will remain active until the first Grand Finale triggers.

## 4. ENHANCED TURN STRUCTURE

The five-phase turn structure from the Core Rules remains intact with modifications and additions to Phases 1 and 2 to accommodate House Vault mechanics, Vogue keyword interactions, and Category checking.

### PHASE 1: TRANSFORMATION (Enhanced)

**House Vault Swaps:**
Once per Phase 1, you may swap one card from your hand with one card from your House Vault. Take a card from your Vault and add it to your hand, then place a card from your hand into your Vault. This swap is optional and can occur before, during, or after equipping cards to your Player Mat.

The Vault swap allows you to bank powerful cards for future turns or retrieve previously stored cards when they become strategically relevant. Cards in the Vault are protected from Grand Finale resets and remain available across multiple Rounds.

**Instant Slay Conditions (Unchanged):**
Instant Slay rules function identically to Core Rules. If your Appeal meets Stage cost during Phase 1 and a card has the Instant Slay keyword, you may claim the Stage immediately and trigger the Grand Finale.

### PHASE 2: THE REVEAL (Enhanced)

Phase 2 gains three additional steps that execute in specific order before the standard Deep Storage check and Fusion/Matching calculations.

**Step 1: Vogue Trigger**
Examine your equipped cards. If any card in your Deep Storage positions has the Vogue keyword, you may swap that Deep Storage card with your current Master card in the same slot. The Vogue card becomes the new Master (moving to the top visible position) while the previous Master moves into Deep Storage beneath it.

This swap occurs before Category checking and before calculating brand bonuses, potentially changing which brand colors are visible for multiplier purposes.

**Step 2: Category Check**
Compare your current equipped Coordinate against the active Category card's requirements. If you meet the specified condition (for example, "Must have Makeup equipped" for Face Face Face, or "Must have NO Camp items" for Executive Realness), gain one tens token from the supply.

Place the tens token in your personal area where you can easily track your accumulated tens. Multiple tens tokens can be gained over many Rounds as Categories change and you rebuild Coordinates to match new judging requirements.

**Step 3: Fusion Logic (With Appeal Clarification)**
Execute the standard Fusion Look check from Core Rules. If your five core slots contain exactly two different brand colors, activate all Fusion ability triggers in Triple Threat boxes.

**Critical Advanced Rule:** The distinction between Master cards and Deep Storage cards for Appeal contribution is emphasized in Advanced Circuit play. Master cards granting Fusion triggers contribute both utility effects and numerical Appeal (★) values. Deep Storage cards granting Fusion triggers contribute utility effects only with zero Appeal contribution regardless of printed values.

**Step 4: Multipliers (Unchanged)**
Apply Matching Brand (×2 for five core slots sharing one brand) or Perfect Illusion (×3 for five core slots matching Queen's Tenet) multipliers exactly as described in Core Rules.

**Step 5: Instant Slay Check (Unchanged)**
If Appeal now meets Stage cost, you may claim the Stage immediately if you have not yet Slayed this Round.

### PHASE 3: SHOPPING (Enhanced)

**Tens Token Redemption:**
At any point during Phase 3 before, during, or after making purchases, you may spend accumulated tens tokens for immediate resources. Each tens token can be converted to your choice of either $2 Tips or +2★ Appeal.

This conversion is permanent and cannot be reversed. Tips gained through tens token conversion are added to your available spending pool for the current Phase 3 only. Appeal gained through tens token conversion is added to your total for potential Slay attempts during Phase 4.

Standard purchasing from the Wardrobe Rack and Thrift Store Throwbacks proceeds normally with any Tips available from equipped cards, Action cards, or tens token conversions.

### PHASE 4 & 5: SLAY OR BATTLE, CLEANUP (Unchanged)

These phases function identically to Core Rules with no Advanced Circuit modifications. The Grand Finale trigger during Phase 4 Slays will proceed to the advanced Grand Finale sequence described below.

## 5. THE BALL HOUSE SYSTEM

### House Mechanics Overview

Ball Houses provide three mechanical benefits to their controlling player. First, each House has a unique passive ability that functions continuously throughout the game with no activation cost. Second, each House specifies a House Vault capacity and may include special Vault rules. Third, some Houses provide conditional bonuses that trigger when specific game state conditions are met.

### House Passive Abilities

House passives vary widely in mechanical identity. Some Houses boost resource generation (gaining bonus Tips when purchasing cards or bonus Appeal when equipping specific brands). Other Houses provide card advantage (drawing extra cards when conditions are met). Combat-focused Houses enhance Dragdagulan battle performance through LS Point bonuses or Realness keyword grants. Economic Houses reduce card costs or provide purchasing discounts.

Players should select Houses that synergize with their chosen Queen character and intended strategic archetype. A player running Kawaii-Bot (who benefits from Matching Brand looks) should prioritize Houses that reward mono-brand builds. A player running Mother Mortis (who plays cards from the Backstage Archive) should consider Houses that facilitate discard pile interaction or recursion loops.

### House Vault Strategic Depth

The House Vault introduces persistent storage that survives Grand Finale resets. This creates new strategic possibilities for banking high-cost cards purchased late in a Round for guaranteed access in the subsequent Round after Coordinates reset. Players can also store situational cards like combat tricks or Stage-specific enablers until the perfect moment arises.

Vault management requires careful planning. Storing too many cards in the Vault means fewer cards cycling through your deck for normal draw. Storing too few cards wastes the Vault's strategic potential. Optimal Vault usage typically involves banking one or two high-impact cards that you want reliable access to while keeping the remainder of your deck churning for consistent resource generation.

### Example Houses

**The Royal House of LaBeija:**
Passive Ability: "Survival — Whenever you lose a Battle, gain $1 Tip (Sympathy Tips)."
House Vault: Standard two-card capacity.
Strategic Identity: Combat-focused house that mitigates battle loss consequences.

**The House of Xtravaganza:**
Passive Ability: "Legacy — You satisfy Realness Requirements for Categories even if you are missing one item."
House Vault: Standard two-card capacity.
Strategic Identity: Category-focused house that eases tens token accumulation.

**The Iconic International House of Miyake-Mugler:**
Passive Ability: "Face — Makeup cards cost -$1 Tip to purchase."
House Vault: Standard two-card capacity.
Strategic Identity: Economic house favoring Face slot strategies.

## 6. CATEGORY DECK & JUDGING

### Category Structure

Category cards function as a parallel challenge system to the Stage Deck. While Stages represent performance opportunities with Appeal thresholds, Categories represent judging themes with specific requirement conditions. Both decks run simultaneously throughout the game with Categories changing during each Grand Finale alongside Stage replacements.

### Category Requirements

Each Category card specifies a condition that must be met with your equipped Coordinate to earn one tens token during Phase 2 Category checking. Requirements vary widely in difficulty and specificity. Some Categories have universal conditions that any Coordinate can satisfy (such as "Must have Makeup equipped" for Face Face Face). Other Categories impose restrictive demands that require specific brands or Coordinate compositions (such as "Must have 2+ Edgy items equipped" for Banjee Girl Realness).

### Tens Token Accumulation

Tens tokens gained through Category satisfaction accumulate indefinitely in your personal play area. Unlike Appeal or Tips which reset at turn or Round end, tens tokens persist across multiple Rounds until you choose to spend them. This creates an economic engine where players who consistently meet Category requirements build resource stockpiles that can be converted into burst Tips or Appeal when needed for critical purchases or Stage Slays.

### Category Examples

**Face Face Face:**
Requirement: Must have a Face card equipped.
Difficulty: Very Easy (any Coordinate with makeup)
Strategic Consideration: Nearly universal completion rate

**Executive Realness:**
Requirement: Must have NO Camp (Yellow) items equipped.
Difficulty: Medium (requires avoiding entire brand)
Strategic Consideration: Mono-brand or dual-brand non-Yellow Coordinates

**Butch Queen Up In Drags:**
Requirement: Must have NO Wigs equipped.
Difficulty: Hard (sacrifices core slot for bonus)
Strategic Consideration: Requires building around missing Wig slot

## 7. TENS TOKENS ECONOMY

### Token Conversion Rates

Tens tokens convert at a fixed exchange rate of one token to either $2 Tips or +2★ Appeal. This conversion can occur at any point during your turn when you have priority to make decisions. Multiple tens tokens can be converted simultaneously during a single conversion action.

The asymmetric conversion rate (one token for two resources) creates interesting valuation decisions. A tens token is worth two Tips which can purchase a low-cost card from the Wardrobe Rack, or two Appeal which might provide the exact threshold needed to Slay a Stage. Players must evaluate current board state, upcoming Stage costs, and available purchase opportunities to optimize tens token conversion timing.

### Strategic Conversion Timing

Early game tens token conversions typically favor Tips to accelerate deck improvement through Wardrobe Rack purchases. Mid-game conversions split between Tips for expensive designer cards and Appeal for Stage Slays as players compete for high-value challenges. Late game conversions often focus on Appeal bursts to reach game-ending Stages before opponents can respond.

Banking tens tokens without converting provides flexibility but risks holding resources that never get spent if the game ends unexpectedly. Converting tens tokens immediately upon earning provides tempo advantage but reduces future optionality if better conversion opportunities emerge later.

### Category Denial Strategies

Some advanced players intentionally build Coordinates that deny common Categories to slow opponent tens token accumulation while prioritizing their own Category satisfaction through House choices or brand focus. This creates an economic advantage where one player accumulates tens tokens at twice the rate of opponents, building an insurmountable resource lead through sustained Category dominance.

## 8. HOUSE VAULT MECHANICS

### Vault Capacity

Standard House Vaults hold up to two cards. Some Houses may modify this capacity to one card, three cards, or impose special restrictions (such as "Vault may only contain Action cards" or "Vault must contain at least one Accessory").

Vault capacity is a hard limit. If your Vault is full and you wish to store a new card, you must first remove one card from the Vault (typically by swapping it into your hand during Phase 1 Vault swap action).

### Vault Persistence

Cards stored in the Vault remain there indefinitely across multiple turns and Rounds. Vault contents are not affected by Grand Finale resets that archive equipped Coordinates. This persistence makes Vaults ideal for storing expensive cards purchased late in Rounds, powerful situational cards that may not be useful every turn, or specific enablers for combo strategies that require multiple pieces to assemble.

### Vault Visibility

Some Houses specify whether Vault contents are public information (face-up and visible to all players) or private information (face-down and hidden from opponents). Standard Houses typically use private Vaults where opponents cannot see what cards you have banked.

Public Vaults create additional information for opponents to use when making strategic decisions about Stage priorities or Dragdagulan targeting. Private Vaults maintain surprise potential but require players to track Vault contents mentally across multiple turns.

### Phase 1 Vault Swap Timing

The once-per-Phase-1 Vault swap can occur at any point during Transformation. You may swap cards before equipping anything, between equipping cards, or after finishing all equipment decisions. This timing flexibility allows you to evaluate your current hand, consider equipment options, bank unused cards for future turns, or retrieve stored cards that have become immediately relevant.

## 9. DEEP STORAGE & VOGUE KEYWORDS

### Enhanced Deep Storage

Deep Storage functions identically to Core Rules with one additional interaction pattern introduced by the Vogue keyword. Deep Storage cards remain buried beneath Masters, contribute brand colors for Fusion checks, activate Fusion ability text, and provide zero numerical Appeal through Fusion triggers.

### Vogue Keyword Swapping

Cards with the Vogue keyword gain a special Phase 2 swap ability. During Step 1 of Phase 2 (The Reveal), before Category checking and before calculating brand multipliers, you may swap any Vogue keyword card in Deep Storage with your current Master card in the same slot.

This swap changes which card is the visible Master for brand matching purposes while moving the previous Master into Deep Storage. The Vogue swap allows you to maintain color flexibility for Fusion triggers (keeping both cards in the slot) while changing which brand is visible for Matching Brand or Perfect Illusion calculations.

### Strategic Vogue Applications

Vogue swapping enables sophisticated brand manipulation strategies. You might equip a Vogue card in Deep Storage early in a Round to preserve a specific brand color for Fusion triggers. Later, when you acquire four other cards matching the Vogue card's brand, you can swap the Vogue card to Master position to achieve Matching Brand multiplier instantly without losing the previous Master's brand contribution to Fusion diversity.

Vogue also allows responding to Category changes during Grand Finales. If the new Category requires a specific brand visibility and you have a Vogue card of that brand in Deep Storage, you can swap it to Master during Phase 2 to immediately satisfy the Category and gain a tens token without equipping a new card.

### Vogue Timing Reminder

Vogue swaps occur during Phase 2 Step 1, which happens before Category checking (Step 2) and before Fusion/Matching calculations (Steps 3-4). This ordering is critical for combo strategies that rely on specific brand visibility patterns at specific phases of turn resolution.

## 10. PERFECT ILLUSION & UNTOUCHABLE

### Perfect Illusion Requirements

Perfect Illusion is achieved when all five of your core slots contain Master cards matching your Queen's signature Tenet brand. For example, Kawaii-Bot has Pink as her signature Tenet. If all five of Kawaii-Bot's core slot Masters are Pink brand cards, she achieves Perfect Illusion.

The requirement specifies Master cards only. Deep Storage cards do not affect Perfect Illusion calculations regardless of their brands. This means you could have diverse colors in Deep Storage for Fusion triggers while maintaining mono-brand Masters for Perfect Illusion multiplier.

### Perfect Illusion Benefits

Perfect Illusion provides two benefits simultaneously. First, your total Appeal is multiplied by three (×3) rather than the standard Matching Brand multiplier of two (×2). Second, you gain Untouchable status for the remainder of the current Round until the next Grand Finale.

### Untouchable Status

Untouchable grants complete immunity from Dragdagulan battles. Opponents cannot target you for combat while you have Untouchable status. If you are the only player with Untouchable in a multiplayer game, the remaining players can still battle each other but must exclude you from combat targeting.

Untouchable status ends when the Round ends through Grand Finale. If you maintain Perfect Illusion through the next Round's Coordinate building, you regain Untouchable status during your next Phase 2 calculations.

### Perfect Illusion Strategy

Perfect Illusion represents the ultimate expression of mono-brand deck specialization. Players pursuing Perfect Illusion strategies must commit heavily to a single Tenet through Wardrobe Rack purchases, House selection, and Queen choice. The payoff is enormous: triple Appeal multipliers enable Slaying high-cost Stages with relatively modest base equipment values, while Untouchable status provides safety from combat losses and penalty accumulation.

However, Perfect Illusion strategies are vulnerable to disruption. If opponents force you to discard cards through battle effects or sabotage abilities, replacing your mono-brand pieces becomes difficult. Category requirements that demand brand diversity also conflict with Perfect Illusion builds, forcing choices between tens token accumulation and Untouchable maintenance.

## 11. ADVANCED GLOSSARY

**Ball House** — Faction card drafted during setup providing passive abilities and House Vault. Remains active throughout the entire game.

**Category** — Judging requirement card that runs parallel to Stages. Meeting Category conditions during Phase 2 awards one tens token. Categories persist until Grand Finale.

**Grand Finale (Advanced)** — Round-ending sequence where all players archive Core Slots, the Stage is claimed, the Category is archived, a new Stage is revealed, and a new Category is revealed.

**House Vault** — Personal storage area holding up to two cards (standard capacity). Vault contents persist through Grand Finale resets. One swap per Phase 1 between hand and Vault.

**Opening the Ball** — Setup procedure for Advanced Circuit. Reveal both Active Stage and Active Category at Round start.

**Perfect Illusion** — State achieved when all five core slot Masters match your Queen's signature Tenet. Grants ×3 Appeal multiplier and Untouchable status.

**Tens Tokens** — Resource earned by meeting Category requirements during Phase 2. Redeemable at any time for $2 Tips or +2★ Appeal per token. Accumulates across Rounds.

**Untouchable** — Status granting immunity from Dragdagulan battles. Gained through Perfect Illusion or certain card abilities. Prevents opponents from targeting you for combat.

**Vogue (Keyword)** — Special ability allowing Deep Storage cards to swap positions with Masters during Phase 2 Step 1. Changes visible brand for multiplier calculations while maintaining brand diversity for Fusion triggers.

## 12. TRANSITIONING FROM CORE RULES

### Learning Curve

Players transitioning from Core Rules to Advanced Circuit should introduce new mechanics gradually over multiple games. For the first Advanced Circuit game, consider implementing only the House system and Vault mechanics while omitting Categories and tens tokens. Once players are comfortable with House abilities and Vault swapping, add the Category Deck and tens token economy. Finally, after several games with all mechanics, players will develop intuition for optimal conversion timing and Vault management.

### Rule Conflicts

When Advanced Circuit rules conflict with Core Rules, Advanced Circuit takes precedence. The enhanced Phase 2 step sequence (Vogue swap, Category check, Fusion logic) replaces the simpler Core Rules Phase 2. All other Core Rules remain in effect unless explicitly modified by Advanced Circuit text.

### Gameplay Duration

Advanced Circuit games typically run longer than Core games due to increased decision complexity and additional resource management. Expect ninety-minute sessions for four-player games with experienced players. New players attempting their first Advanced Circuit game may require two hours.

### Strategic Depth

The Advanced Circuit dramatically increases strategic depth by introducing pre-game drafting (House selection), economic engines (tens token accumulation), persistent storage (House Vault), and information asymmetry (hidden Vault contents, Category predictions). Players who master these systems will develop significant skill advantages through optimal resource conversion, clever Vault timing, and anticipatory Category building.
